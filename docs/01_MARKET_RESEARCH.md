# 01 — Market Research & Opportunity

## 1. Executive summary

FactoryOS targets small and growing Indian manufacturing businesses whose daily operations are coordinated through paper registers, spreadsheets, phone calls and messaging applications. The product hypothesis is that owners will pay for a simpler operating layer that makes production, orders, stock, attendance and breakdowns visible without deploying a full ERP.

The strongest initial positioning is not “ERP replacement”. It is:

**A simple factory operations system for owners and managers.**

## 2. Market evidence

### Gujarat MSME base

The Ministry of MSME state dashboard reports Gujarat's combined Udyam and Udyam Assist Platform count at approximately 54.2 lakh enterprises in the February 2026 snapshot. The count includes informal micro enterprises and businesses across sectors; it must not be interpreted as the number of manufacturing factories or FactoryOS prospects.

A Gujarat Chamber of Commerce ecosystem report states that, as of 18 June 2025, Gujarat had 25,35,932 Udyam-registered MSMEs. Differences between sources and dates reflect different coverage and reporting snapshots.

GIDC's industrial-estate presentation identifies 225 GIDC estates and describes clusters across engineering, brass parts, chemicals, petrochemicals, textiles, ceramics and other industries.

### Adoption barrier evidence

A 2026 research paper on digital transformation barriers in Indian manufacturing SMEs identifies high investment, uncertainty about return on investment and lack of multiskilled workforces as crucial barriers. This supports a product strategy based on low setup effort, simple workflows, low training burden and visible ROI.

### Competitive evidence

Zoho ERP's India pricing page lists manufacturing operations, shop-floor production, work centres, inventory and quality features. Standard pricing is displayed from ₹999 per user/month billed annually, with other employee pricing and plan conditions shown on its official page. This is evidence that established vendors address the category; it is not a like-for-like comparison with FactoryOS pricing.

ERPNext, Odoo and Tally-based workflows are relevant alternatives. Competitor evaluation must include actual onboarding, implementation, language support, mobile usability, offline behaviour and total cost—not just licence price.

## 3. Geographic beachhead

Prioritise discovery and pilot sales in:
- Ahmedabad: engineering, fabrication, packaging and plastics.
- Rajkot: engineering components, machine shops, casting and brass.
- Surat: textile-related operations and packaging.
- Vadodara: engineering, chemicals and pharma-adjacent manufacturing.
- Vapi: chemicals and plastics.
- Morbi: ceramics and associated supply chains.

These are prospecting hypotheses based on industrial concentration, not measured demand for FactoryOS. Confirm cluster-specific workflows by visiting factories and speaking with owners and supervisors.

## 4. Initial ideal customer profile

### ICP A — Owner-led small factory

- 10–30 workers.
- One or two supervisors.
- One shift initially, with possible second shift.
- Owner asks for updates several times a day.
- Production and stock data live in registers, spreadsheets or WhatsApp.
- At least one smartphone and intermittent internet.
- Repeated orders or recurring production jobs.
- No full-time IT administrator.

### Disqualify or defer initially

- Highly regulated process plants requiring validated systems.
- Factories requiring PLC/SCADA integration on day one.
- Businesses needing full accounting, payroll, procurement and statutory ERP.
- Plants with complex multi-level BOMs and advanced MRP needs.
- Factories unwilling to appoint a responsible manager/data-entry operator.

## 5. Jobs to be done

### Owner
- “When I am away from the factory, show me whether production is on track.”
- “Tell me which orders are at risk and why.”
- “Show me where machines stopped and how long they were down.”
- “Tell me when material is running low.”
- “Give me one reliable end-of-day summary.”
- “Let me see what the manager has updated and what is still missing.”

### Manager
- “Let me record production quickly without filling a complicated form.”
- “Show me today's jobs and priorities.”
- “Let me report a breakdown with a reason and status.”
- “Let me record material use and finished output.”
- “Tell me what information is still due before the day closes.”

## 6. Pain-to-feature prioritisation

| Pain | Feature | MVP priority |
|---|---|---|
| No reliable daily output | Production entry and target tracking | P0 |
| Owner cannot see order status | Orders and order progress | P0 |
| Updates are scattered | Owner dashboard and activity feed | P0 |
| Breakdowns not logged | Machine downtime log | P1 |
| Material stock uncertain | Basic raw/finished inventory | P1 |
| Attendance in paper register | Attendance and shift summary | P1 |
| Defects not measured | Quality/rejection entry | P1 |
| Dispatch status unclear | Dispatch register | P1 |
| Complex costing and MRP | Advanced production planning | Later |
| Payroll and statutory filings | Payroll/compliance integrations | Out of MVP |

## 7. Competitor landscape

| Alternative | What customers may value | FactoryOS response |
|---|---|---|
| Paper registers + calls | Familiar, no subscription | Show quick wins and simple migration |
| Excel / Google Sheets | Flexible, inexpensive | Structured workflows, reminders and dashboards |
| WhatsApp | Already adopted | Turn updates into structured operational records |
| TallyPrime | Accounting familiarity | Integrate/export; do not compete on accounting |
| ERPNext | Broad ERP, open-source | Faster guided setup and focused workflow |
| Odoo | Modular, extensive | Narrow, easy-to-learn owner/manager experience |
| Zoho ERP | Broad business and manufacturing features | Focus on micro/small factory simplicity and local onboarding |

Do not claim that competitors are difficult without direct usability testing. Run comparative task tests with real managers.

## 8. Differentiation hypothesis

1. Two-role experience rather than an employee-heavy ERP.
2. Owner-first command centre with “what needs attention?” priorities.
3. Manager task flows designed for a phone held on a factory floor.
4. Gujarati and Hindi language support.
5. Quick-entry forms, defaults, templates and QR/short-code machine identification later.
6. Daily digest with clear data provenance and missing-data warnings.
7. Guided onboarding and human-assisted setup.
8. Transparent pricing by factory/site or operational capacity, not confusing module add-ons.

## 9. Market validation plan

Interview at least:
- 8 owners.
- 8 managers/supervisors.
- 4 factory accountants/admin staff if attendance is in scope.

Observe a real shift handover and end-of-day reporting. Ask to see blank/consented examples of registers and spreadsheets; do not collect employee personal data unnecessarily.

Validate:
- How production is counted.
- Whether production is by piece, kg, batch, metre, litre or another unit.
- Whether one order can contain multiple products/operations.
- How scrap, rework and rejected output are recorded.
- How stock is issued and reconciled.
- Who can correct yesterday's entries.
- What happens during power/network failure.
- Whether the manager owns a smartphone and can use it at work.
- What the owner currently pays for software, accountant/admin time or manual reporting.
- Whether the owner will pay ₹1,499–₹5,999 per month after a pilot.

### Go/no-go signals

Proceed to paid pilot if:
- At least 10 of 15 interviewed factories describe recurring visibility/reporting pain.
- At least 5 agree to test a prototype with their real workflow.
- At least 3 agree to a paid pilot or deposit.
- A manager can complete the core production entry in under 60 seconds after training.
- Owner can interpret the daily report without explanation.

## 11. Research limitations

- Udyam totals are not the same as addressable manufacturing factories.
- Industrial cluster lists do not prove willingness to pay.
- Competitor pricing changes and may exclude onboarding, tax, implementation or add-ons.
- No primary customer interviews have yet been conducted for this blueprint.
- Pricing and adoption assumptions must be tested with local factories.
